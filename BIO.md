# Inha Cha

Inha Cha is a PhD student at Georgia Tech working with [Dr. Richmond Y. Wong](https://richmondywong.com/) and [Dr. Carl DiSalvo](https://carldisalvo.com/). Her research sits at the intersection of human-computer interaction (HCI), science and technology studies (STS), and technology policy. She studies how AI is reshaping designers’ everyday work and how designers negotiate those changes.

Her broader research examines how the promise of innovation makes AI adoption seem inevitable, obscuring its costs, trade-offs, and uncertainties. She looks at how organizations and governments make AI a priority and how that shapes decisions about whether, when, and how to use it. Through her research, she asks how a handful of tech companies came to hold so much influence over the future of work and what workers can do together to build alternatives.[^research-detail]

[^research-detail]: She critically examines the lifecycle of AI systems, focusing on how innovation-driven narratives frame AI progress as inevitable and obscure the costs, trade-offs, and uncertainties of adoption. Using qualitative, interpretive, and design methods, she empirically explores how organizations and nation-states mobilize AI as a strategic priority, and how these ambitions influence decisions around adoption, refusal, and deliberation in practice. Her work focuses on:

    - when, why, and how AI is adopted, refused, or deliberated within organizational settings.
    - how national strategic priorities are constructed and justified through discourses of “AI sovereignty.”

    By tracing the social, organizational, and political conditions that shape AI adoption, she identifies opportunities to foster more deliberate and responsible approaches to AI within complex institutional environments.
