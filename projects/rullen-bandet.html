Each instrument uses a different type of interaction, such as pressing, touching, or shaking, so children can participate in ways that work for them without needing prior musical experience. - type: video provider: google_drive video_id: "1LreB0_k8xiWXfRrIBI5u1mx0bh12i07X" video_title: "Rullen Bandet instrument demonstration" - type: image image: /assets/projectimages/rullen-bandet/detail-01.png alt: "Four Rullen Bandet instruments: touch tambourine, piano, shake tambourine, and guitar" width: 2015 height: 638 - type: text text: >- We designed four simplified instruments: a piano played by pressing buttons, a drum activated by touch, a tambourine played by shaking, and a guitar combining touch and button presses. All four instruments are connected to a central unit containing a speaker, battery, and Bela microcontroller. Each instrument controls a separate musical track, and all tracks stay in sync. When someone plays an instrument, its track becomes audible, making it easier for children to play music with others without having to coordinate the timing themselves. - type: image image: /assets/projectimages/rullen-bandet/playing-instruments.png alt: "Demonstrations of playing the Rullen Bandet musical instruments" width: 1058 height: 608 - type: text text: >- Before designing the instruments, we explored universal design, inclusive design, and accessible digital musical instruments (ADMIs). We focused on making the instruments easy to understand and use, with simple interactions that require minimal physical effort. Due to COVID-19 restrictions, we couldn't test the instruments with our intended participants. Instead, we tried the working prototypes with classmates to see how the instruments could be played in a group setting. - type: video provider: google_drive video_id: "1iy8Bcb_QW_nALHBVDCUD8Wkj6SpLuyyA" video_title: "Rullen Bandet group prototype demonstration" ---
